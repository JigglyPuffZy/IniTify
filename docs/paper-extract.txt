THE PROBLEM AND ITS BACKGROUND
BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY
Extreme heat has become an increasingly serious public health concern as climate change continues to raise global temperatures and increase the frequency and intensity of heat waves. Prolonged exposure to extreme heat can lead to dehydration, heat exhaustion, heat stroke, cardiovascular complications, and other heat-related illnesses, particularly among older adults, outdoor workers, children, and individuals with pre-existing health conditions. As heat-related health risks continue to rise, there is a growing need for innovative technologies that can monitor environmental conditions, assess individual risk, and provide timely interventions to prevent serious health consequences.
Tropical and urban areas are especially vulnerable due to high temperatures, humidity, and the urban heat island effect, which intensify heat exposure. In the Philippines, extreme heat has become more frequent, resulting in class suspensions, increased health risks, and disruptions to daily activities. Although weather agencies issue heat index advisories, these warnings are generally intended for the public as a whole and may not accurately reflect an individual's actual level of risk. Factors such as age, existing medical conditions, activity level, hydration status, and localized environmental conditions can significantly influence a person's susceptibility to heat-related illnesses.
Current heat-monitoring applications and warning systems primarily focus on reporting weather conditions or heat index values. While these systems help increase public awareness, many provide limited personalized risk assessment and lack emergency response capabilities when a user may already be experiencing a heat-related emergency. As a result, users may receive warnings but still have limited guidance or assistance during critical situations when immediate action is necessary.
To address these gaps, this study proposes IniTify, a hybrid AI-based heat-risk monitoring and personalized alert system designed to provide intelligent, real-time heat-risk assessment through a mobile application. The system retrieves weather and heat index data from DOST-PAGASA, validates and processes the collected information, and combines it with individual risk factors to generate personalized heat-risk assessments and safety recommendations. By integrating environmental monitoring with artificial intelligence, IniTify aims to provide more accurate and relevant alerts than conventional heat advisories.
A key innovation of the proposed system is its Emergency Assistance System, which extends beyond conventional heat alerts by providing automated support during potential heat-related emergencies. The feature activates only when multiple indicators suggest that the user may be experiencing a serious heat-related condition, such as an extreme heat-risk level, repeated failure to respond to safety prompts, and prolonged inactivity at the same location. Once these conditions are met, the application automatically notifies the user's designated emergency contact with the user's name, current risk level, last known location, the nearest hospital or emergency medical facility, and the estimated travel time. At the same time, the application displays an emergency assistance screen containing navigation to the nearest hospital, emergency contact information, and basic first-aid instructions for heat stroke while medical assistance is on the way. This multi-condition approach helps reduce false alarms while ensuring that users receive timely assistance during emergencies.
Furthermore, IniTify incorporates offline data caching to maintain access to previously retrieved heat-risk information and essential emergency features even when internet connectivity is temporarily unavailable. By combining real-time environmental monitoring, AI-based personalized risk assessment, intelligent recommendations, emergency assistance, and offline functionality within a single mobile platform, the proposed system aims to improve early detection, preparedness, and emergency response to heat-related illnesses.
Ultimately, this study seeks to contribute to the development of smarter, technology-driven public health solutions that enhance community resilience against extreme heat. The proposed system also supports Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 3: Good Health and Well-being and SDG 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities by promoting preventive healthcare, improving emergency preparedness, and strengthening climate change adaptation efforts in the Philippines.

Figure 2 presents the system framework of the proposed IniTify mobile application. The system retrieves real-time heat index data from DOST-PAGASA, which is cleaned and validated before processing. SCOPE AND DELIMITATIONS
This study focuses on the design, development, and testing of IniTify, a hybrid AI-based heat-risk monitoring and personalized alert system that integrates official heat index data from DOST-PAGASA with individual risk factors to provide personalized heat-risk assessments and early warnings against heat-related illnesses. The system consists of a mobile application that retrieves real-time heat index data through an internet connection and generates personalized heat-risk levels based on environmental conditions and user information, including age, health condition, activity level, hydration status, and GPS location.
The study covers the development of the system's core components, including automated heat data retrieval, data processing, AI-based heat-risk assessment using a Decision Tree model, personalized safety recommendations, real-time push notifications, an Emergency Assistance System, and offline data caching. The Emergency Assistance System is designed to activate when multiple conditions indicate a possible heat-related emergency. Once activated, it automatically sends the user's last known location, current heat-risk level, and the nearest hospital information to the designated emergency contact while providing hospital navigation and basic first-aid guidance through the mobile application. The developed system will undergo functional testing under environmental conditions in Tuguegarao City, Cagayan, to verify its ability to perform its intended functions.
However, the study has several delimitations. IniTify is intended only as a decision-support and early warning tool and is not a medical diagnostic or treatment device. The system relies on the availability and accuracy of real-time heat index data from DOST-PAGASA, the correctness of the information provided by the user, and the user's permission to access location services. Although the application includes offline data caching, features such as real-time heat-risk updates, emergency notifications, and hospital navigation require an active internet connection. In addition, the Emergency Assistance System depends on the user having a registered emergency contact and location services enabled. The study is limited to the development and functional testing of the mobile application within the study period and under the environmental conditions of Tuguegarao City, Cagayan; therefore, the results may not be applicable to other locations or environmental settings.

Artificial Intelligence (AI). The technology integrated into the IniTify system that uses a Decision Tree model to analyze environmental and individual data for estimating heat-related risk and generating personalized recommendations.
Data Cleaning and Validation. The process of checking, organizing, and verifying retrieved heat index data to ensure its accuracy and reliability before it is analyzed by the system.
Decision Tree Model. A machine learning algorithm used by the IniTify system to classify a user's heat-risk level based on environmental conditions and individual risk factors.

Heat Index. A measure that combines air temperature and relative humidity to represent the apparent temperature experienced by the user.
Heat-Related Risk. The likelihood that a user may experience harmful effects from heat exposure based on environmental conditions and individual risk factors analyzed by the IniTify system.
Hybrid Monitoring System. A system that combines real-time environmental heat data and individual risk factors within a single mobile application to provide personalized heat-risk assessments.
Individual Risk Factors. Personal characteristics considered by the IniTify system in estimating heat-related risk, including age, health condition, activity level, and hydration status.
Mobile Application. The smartphone-based platform through which the IniTify system retrieves heat index data, processes user information, generates heat-risk assessments, displays alerts, and provides emergency assistance.

REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND RELATED STUDIES
REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE (RRL)
Theoretical Foundations of the Study
The increasing frequency and intensity of extreme heat events due to climate change have highlighted the need for more advanced systems that can support heat-risk prevention and public health preparedness. Traditional heat warning systems primarily issue generalized advisories based on meteorological conditions. However, recent developments emphasize the importance of integrating environmental information with individual risk factors to provide more personalized and effective heat-risk assessments. Heat-health warning systems, artificial intelligence (AI), and mobile health (mHealth) technologies have therefore become essential tools in improving early detection, timely communication, and emergency response to heat-related illnesses (World Health Organization [WHO], 2026). 
The development of IniTify is anchored on three theoretical foundations: the Input–Process–Output (IPO) Model, the Heat Health Warning System (HHWS) Framework, and the Decision Tree-Based Decision Support Framework. These theories explain how environmental and personal information are transformed into personalized heat-risk assessments, timely alerts, and emergency assistance.
Input–Process–Output (IPO) Model
The Input–Process–Output (IPO) Model serves as the primary systems development framework of this study. The model explains that every information system receives inputs, processes them through organized procedures, and generates outputs that achieve a desired objective. Because of its logical and systematic structure, the IPO Model has been widely applied in systems analysis and information systems development (Laudon & Laudon, 2022).
In the proposed IniTify system, the inputs include real-time heat index data retrieved from DOST-PAGASA and user-specific information such as age, health condition, activity level, hydration status, and GPS location. These inputs undergo several processes, including automated data retrieval, data cleaning and validation, Decision Tree-based heat-risk analysis, personalized recommendation generation, and emergency condition assessment. The processed information produces outputs, including personalized heat-risk classifications, real-time heat alerts, safety recommendations, emergency contact notification, hospital navigation, first-aid guidance, and offline access to essential emergency information. Guided by the IPO Model, the IniTify system follows a structured process that transforms environmental and personal data into meaningful information that supports heat-risk prevention and emergency response.
Heat Health Warning System (HHWS) Framework
The Heat Health Warning System (HHWS) Framework provides the public health foundation of this study. According to the World Health Organization (WHO) and the World Meteorological Organization (WMO), Heat Health Warning Systems integrate meteorological information with health risk assessments, communication strategies, and preparedness measures to reduce heat-related illnesses and deaths during periods of extreme heat. These systems are intended not only to provide timely warnings but also to encourage preventive actions before adverse health effects occur (WHO & WMO, 2016). 
Recent WHO guidance further emphasizes that effective heat-health action plans should incorporate early warning systems, identify vulnerable populations, strengthen communication strategies, and improve emergency preparedness to reduce the health impacts of extreme heat (WHO Regional Office for Europe, 2026). 
IniTify adopts the principles of the HHWS Framework by integrating official heat index data from DOST-PAGASA with individual risk factors to generate personalized heat-risk assessments. Through real-time alerts, precautionary recommendations, and emergency assistance features, the system supports early detection of heat-related risks and promotes timely preventive actions among individuals exposed to extreme heat.
Decision Tree-Based Decision Support Framework
The Decision Tree-Based Decision Support Framework serves as the intelligent decision-making foundation of this study. Decision Trees are among the most widely used classification techniques in artificial intelligence because they classify data using a sequence of logical decision rules. Their transparent and interpretable structure makes them particularly suitable for healthcare applications where decisions should be understandable and explainable (Breiman et al., 1984; Quinlan, 1986).
In IniTify, the Decision Tree model analyzes real-time heat index data together with individual risk factors—including age, health condition, activity level, hydration status, and GPS location—to classify users into different heat-risk levels. Based on the resulting classification, the system generates personalized safety recommendations and real-time alerts. When multiple indicators suggest a possible heat-related emergency, the system activates its Emergency Assistance System, which automatically notifies the designated emergency contact, identifies the nearest hospital, provides navigation assistance, and displays first-aid guidance while awaiting medical assistance. This application of Decision Tree analysis is consistent with recent studies showing that machine learning techniques can improve heat-health warning systems by providing more accurate and data-driven risk classification for early intervention. 
Key Concepts 
2.1 Climate Change, Extreme Heat, and Heat-Related Illnesses
Climate change has contributed to a significant increase in global temperatures, resulting in more frequent, prolonged, and intense heat events. According to the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC, 2023), human-induced climate change has increased the likelihood of extreme heat occurrences across many regions of the world. Extreme heat poses serious public health risks, particularly for older adults, children, outdoor workers, and individuals with pre-existing medical conditions.
Heat-related illnesses occur when the body is unable to regulate its internal temperature due to prolonged exposure to high temperatures. These illnesses range from mild conditions such as heat cramps and heat exhaustion to severe and life-threatening conditions such as heat stroke. The World Health Organization (2024) identifies extreme heat as one of the leading weather-related causes of illness and death, emphasizing the importance of early warning systems and preventive interventions to reduce heat-related health risks. As climate change continues to intensify heat events, innovative technologies capable of providing timely warnings and personalized recommendations have become increasingly important in protecting public health (WHO, 2024).
2.2 Heat Index and Official Meteorological Data
The heat index is a measure of the apparent temperature experienced by the human body, combining air temperature and relative humidity to estimate how hot the environment feels. High humidity reduces the body's ability to cool itself through sweating, making the perceived temperature higher than the actual air temperature. Because of this, the heat index is widely used by meteorological agencies and public health organizations to assess heat-related health risks and issue appropriate heat advisories (National Weather Service [NWS], n.d.).
In the Philippines, the Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (DOST-PAGASA) serves as the official government agency responsible for monitoring weather conditions and issuing heat index advisories. These advisories provide timely information regarding potentially dangerous heat conditions and are used by government agencies, schools, and the public to prepare for periods of extreme heat (DOST-PAGASA, 2024). IniTify utilizes real-time heat index data from DOST-PAGASA to ensure that heat-risk assessments are based on accurate, reliable, and officially recognized meteorological information.
2.3 Individual Risk Factors in Heat Exposure
Although environmental conditions significantly influence heat exposure, individual characteristics also affect a person's susceptibility to heat-related illnesses. Factors such as age, health condition, physical activity level, and hydration status influence the body's ability to regulate temperature and respond to heat stress. Older adults, individuals with chronic illnesses, physically active persons, and those experiencing dehydration are generally at greater risk of developing heat-related illnesses during periods of extreme heat (World Health Organization, 2024).
IniTify incorporates these individual risk factors into its heat-risk assessment process. By combining personal information with real-time environmental conditions, the system generates personalized heat-risk classifications and safety recommendations that are more appropriate to each user's condition than generalized heat advisories.
2.4 Decision Tree for Heat-Risk Assessment
A Decision Tree is a supervised machine learning algorithm used for classification and prediction by organizing data into a tree-like structure consisting of decision nodes and outcome branches. Each decision is based on specific conditions or attributes, allowing the algorithm to classify data into predefined categories (Breiman et al., 1984; Quinlan, 1986).
Decision Trees are widely used in healthcare because they provide transparent and interpretable decision-making processes. Unlike some machine learning algorithms that function as "black boxes," Decision Trees clearly show how each decision is made, making them suitable for applications where users and developers need understandable results. In IniTify, the Decision Tree model analyzes official heat index data together with individual risk factors to classify users into Low, Moderate, High, or Extreme heat-risk levels. Based on the resulting classification, the system generates personalized alerts, precautionary recommendations, and emergency assistance when necessary.
2.5 Mobile Health (mHealth) Applications
Mobile health, commonly referred to as mHealth, involves the use of mobile devices and wireless technologies to support healthcare services, health monitoring, disease prevention, and health promotion. The World Health Organization recognizes mHealth as an important component of digital health because it improves access to health information, facilitates timely communication, and supports public health interventions through mobile technologies (WHO, 2019).
IniTify is developed as an mHealth application that enables users to receive personalized heat-risk assessments, real-time alerts, and safety recommendations directly through their smartphones. By utilizing mobile technology, the system promotes timely access to heat-related information and encourages preventive actions during periods of extreme heat.
2.6 GPS-Based Location Services
Global Positioning System (GPS) technology enables mobile applications to determine a user's geographic location accurately. GPS-based location services are widely used in navigation, emergency response, disaster management, and healthcare applications because they provide real-time location information necessary for timely assistance (Android Developers, n.d.).
In IniTify, GPS is used to determine the user's current location, identify the nearest hospital during heat-related emergencies, provide navigation assistance, and transmit the user's last known location to the designated emergency contact when emergency conditions are detected. This functionality enhances the system's ability to provide immediate assistance during extreme heat events.
2.7 Emergency Assistance Systems
Emergency assistance systems are designed to provide immediate support during critical situations by facilitating communication, navigation, and access to emergency services. Mobile-based emergency systems commonly include emergency contact notification, location sharing, hospital navigation, and first-aid guidance to improve response time and increase the likelihood of timely medical intervention.
The Emergency Assistance System integrated into IniTify activates only when multiple indicators suggest a possible heat-related emergency. Once activated, the system automatically notifies the user's designated emergency contact, shares the user's last known location, identifies the nearest hospital, provides navigation assistance, and displays first-aid instructions while medical assistance is being sought. This integrated approach supports timely response and strengthens user safety during severe heat exposure.
2.8 Offline Data Cache
Offline data caching refers to the temporary storage of essential application data on a mobile device, allowing users to access critical information even when internet connectivity is unavailable. Offline functionality improves the reliability of mobile applications, particularly during emergencies or network disruptions, by ensuring that important information remains accessible.
In IniTify, offline data caching stores essential emergency information, including first-aid guidance and previously retrieved heat-risk information. This feature allows users to access critical safety information even when real-time internet connectivity is temporarily unavailable, improving the system's reliability during emergency situations.
Relevant Literature
3.1 Heat Health Warning Systems
Heat Health Warning Systems (HHWS) have become an essential public health strategy for reducing the impacts of extreme heat on vulnerable populations. These systems combine meteorological forecasts with health-based information to provide timely warnings that encourage individuals and communities to take preventive measures before dangerous heat conditions occur. According to the World Health Organization (WHO) and the World Meteorological Organization (WMO), effective HHWS should include reliable weather monitoring, risk communication, public awareness, and coordinated emergency response to minimize heat-related illnesses and deaths (WHO & WMO, 2015).
Recent studies have emphasized the need to improve traditional heat warning systems by making them more personalized and responsive to individual risk factors. Ebi et al. (2021) explained that conventional heat advisories often provide generalized warnings based solely on weather conditions, which may not adequately represent the varying levels of heat vulnerability among different individuals. The study recommended integrating personal characteristics such as age, health condition, and activity level into heat-health warning systems to improve risk communication and encourage more appropriate protective actions.
Similarly, Boeckmann and Rohn (2021) reported that personalized heat warning systems are more effective than generalized advisories because they consider differences in individual susceptibility to heat exposure. Their findings showed that providing users with risk-specific recommendations increases awareness and promotes preventive behaviors, particularly among high-risk groups such as older adults and outdoor workers.
Advances in digital technology have also improved the implementation of heat-health warning systems through mobile applications. A review by the World Health Organization Regional Office for Europe (2026) highlighted that modern heat-health action plans increasingly utilize mobile technologies to deliver real-time alerts, location-specific information, and personalized recommendations. These digital approaches improve the accessibility of heat-risk information and enable users to respond more quickly to changing environmental conditions.
The reviewed literature demonstrates that current Heat Health Warning Systems are evolving from generalized weather advisories toward personalized, technology-assisted approaches that integrate environmental information with individual characteristics. However, many existing systems primarily focus on issuing warnings and public advisories without incorporating intelligent risk classification, emergency assistance, or integrated mobile-based decision support. The proposed IniTify system addresses these gaps by combining official heat index data from DOST-PAGASA, individual risk factors, Decision Tree-based heat-risk analysis, and emergency assistance features within a single mobile application to provide more personalized and actionable heat-risk management.
3.2 Decision Tree Applications in Healthcare and Risk Prediction
Decision Trees are among the most widely used machine learning algorithms for classification and prediction because of their simplicity, transparency, and interpretability. Unlike more complex artificial intelligence models, Decision Trees classify data by applying a sequence of logical rules, making it easier for developers and users to understand how decisions are generated (Quinlan, 1986). Their ability to produce clear decision paths has made them widely applicable in healthcare, where explainable and reliable decision-making is essential.
In the healthcare sector, Decision Trees have been successfully applied to disease diagnosis, patient risk assessment, and clinical decision support. A review by Tohka and van Gils (2021) explained that interpretable machine learning algorithms such as Decision Trees are valuable in health-related applications because they allow healthcare professionals to understand how predictions are made while maintaining acceptable predictive performance. Their transparency also increases user trust compared to complex "black-box" algorithms.
Similarly, Sidey-Gibbons and Sidey-Gibbons (2019) reported that Decision Trees are effective in predicting health outcomes and classifying patient risk using multiple health-related variables. Their study emphasized that Decision Trees are particularly useful when decision rules need to be easily interpreted, making them suitable for healthcare applications that require timely intervention and understandable recommendations.
Recent studies have also highlighted the growing role of machine learning in environmental health and climate-related risk assessment. Artificial intelligence models have been used to analyze environmental conditions together with individual health information to support early warning systems and improve risk prediction. These intelligent systems enable more accurate classification of health risks and provide personalized recommendations based on both environmental and personal factors (World Health Organization, 2021).
The reviewed literature demonstrates that Decision Trees provide an effective and explainable approach for health-risk classification and decision support. However, most existing healthcare applications focus on disease diagnosis or clinical prediction and are not specifically designed to assess heat-related health risks. Moreover, few studies integrate official meteorological data, individual risk factors, and emergency response features into a single mobile application. The proposed IniTify system addresses these gaps by utilizing a Decision Tree algorithm to analyze official heat index data from DOST-PAGASA together with user-specific information, enabling personalized heat-risk assessment, real-time safety recommendations, and automated emergency assistance during extreme heat events.
3.3 Mobile Health (mHealth) Applications
Mobile health (mHealth) refers to the use of mobile devices, such as smartphones and tablets, to deliver healthcare services, monitor health conditions, and promote disease prevention. With the widespread use of mobile technologies, mHealth has become an important component of digital healthcare by providing users with convenient access to health information, real-time monitoring, and personalized health interventions. According to the World Health Organization (2019), mHealth applications enhance healthcare accessibility and strengthen health systems by supporting health promotion, disease surveillance, and timely communication between users and healthcare providers.
Recent studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of mobile health applications in improving disease prevention and encouraging healthy behaviors. Marcolino et al. (2018) found that mHealth applications contribute to better health outcomes by providing users with timely reminders, personalized recommendations, and continuous monitoring of health conditions. Similarly, a systematic review by Wang et al. (2023) reported that mobile health technologies significantly improve users' adherence to preventive health practices through real-time notifications and personalized feedback, making them valuable tools for public health interventions.
In climate-related health management, mobile applications have increasingly been utilized to provide heat alerts, environmental information, and health recommendations during extreme weather events. These applications enable users to receive location-specific information, monitor environmental conditions, and take preventive measures before heat-related illnesses occur. The World Health Organization (2024) emphasized that digital technologies, including mobile applications, play an important role in strengthening climate resilience by improving public awareness, supporting early warning systems, and promoting timely responses to heat-related risks.
The reviewed literature indicates that mobile health applications are effective platforms for delivering personalized health information and improving preventive healthcare. However, many existing applications focus primarily on health monitoring or general wellness and do not integrate official meteorological data, intelligent heat-risk assessment, emergency response features, and personalized recommendations into a single platform. The proposed IniTify system addresses these limitations by combining official heat index data from DOST-PAGASA, Decision Tree-based heat-risk assessment, personalized safety recommendations, GPS-enabled emergency assistance, and offline emergency information within one mobile application. Through these integrated features, IniTify aims to enhance users' preparedness and response to extreme heat events.
3.4 GPS-Based Location Services in Mobile Health Applications
Global Positioning System (GPS) technology has become an essential component of mobile health (mHealth) applications by enabling location-aware services that support health monitoring, emergency response, and navigation. GPS allows mobile applications to determine a user's geographic location in real time, making it possible to deliver location-specific information and services. According to the Android Developers documentation (Google, n.d.), location services combine GPS, Wi-Fi, cellular networks, and sensors to provide accurate positioning while optimizing battery consumption.
Recent studies have demonstrated the importance of GPS technology in healthcare and emergency management. Ramesh et al. (2023) explained that GPS-enabled mobile applications improve emergency response by allowing users' locations to be shared with healthcare providers and emergency contacts, reducing response time during medical emergencies. Similarly, Boulos et al. (2011) emphasized that location-based services have significantly improved healthcare delivery by supporting navigation, disease surveillance, emergency management, and public health interventions.
Location-based technologies are also increasingly used in disaster preparedness and climate-related applications. During emergencies, GPS enables users to identify nearby healthcare facilities, receive location-specific alerts, and navigate safely to evacuation sites or medical centers. Such capabilities enhance situational awareness and facilitate timely access to healthcare services, particularly during environmental hazards and extreme weather events (World Health Organization, 2024).
The reviewed literature demonstrates that GPS technology plays a significant role in improving emergency response and delivering location-based healthcare services. However, many existing mobile applications use GPS primarily for navigation and do not integrate it with intelligent health-risk assessment and emergency activation. The proposed IniTify system addresses this limitation by integrating GPS technology with Decision Tree-based heat-risk analysis. When the system detects a possible heat-related emergency, it automatically retrieves the user's current location, identifies the nearest hospital, provides navigation assistance, and shares the user's last known location with the designated emergency contact. These integrated functions strengthen emergency preparedness and support timely medical intervention during extreme heat events.
3.5 Emergency Assistance and Hospital Navigation Systems
Emergency assistance systems have become an important component of modern mobile health applications by enabling timely communication and immediate support during medical emergencies. These systems are designed to reduce response time by automatically notifying emergency contacts, providing location information, and directing users to nearby healthcare facilities. According to the World Health Organization (2021), digital health technologies can strengthen emergency preparedness and improve access to healthcare services by facilitating rapid communication and informed decision-making during emergencies.
Recent studies have shown that integrating emergency assistance features into mobile applications enhances users' safety and improves emergency response. Martínez-Pérez et al. (2015) reported that mobile health applications equipped with emergency notification and location-sharing capabilities enable faster medical intervention by providing responders with essential information, such as the user's location and health status. Similarly, Kringle et al. (2020) emphasized that emergency alert systems combined with real-time location services improve coordination among patients, caregivers, and healthcare providers, particularly during time-sensitive medical situations.
Hospital navigation has also become an important feature of emergency healthcare applications. Location-based navigation systems help users identify the nearest healthcare facility and provide optimized travel directions, reducing delays in obtaining medical assistance. Google Maps Platform (Google, n.d.) supports location-aware applications by enabling developers to integrate mapping, route guidance, and nearby healthcare facility searches into mobile applications. Such technologies have been widely adopted in digital health systems to improve accessibility and emergency response.
The reviewed literature indicates that emergency assistance and hospital navigation technologies significantly improve users' access to emergency care by facilitating communication and providing accurate location-based guidance. However, many existing applications activate emergency features manually, requiring users to request assistance themselves. This may be difficult or impossible during severe heat-related emergencies when individuals become unconscious or unable to use their mobile devices. The proposed IniTify system addresses this limitation by automatically activating its Emergency Assistance System when multiple indicators suggest a possible heat-related emergency. Once activated, the system sends the user's last known location and heat-risk level to the designated emergency contact, identifies the nearest hospital, provides navigation assistance, and displays first-aid guidance while waiting for medical assistance. These integrated features enhance emergency preparedness and support timely intervention during extreme heat events.
3.6 Offline Mobile Technologies
Offline functionality has become an essential feature of modern mobile applications, particularly those designed for emergency response and public health. Offline mobile technologies enable applications to continue providing access to essential information and selected features even when internet connectivity is unavailable. By storing important data locally on the user's device, offline systems improve reliability and ensure that critical information remains accessible during emergencies or network interruptions (Google Developers, n.d.).
The World Health Organization (2021) emphasizes that digital health technologies should be designed to remain functional in various operating conditions, including areas with limited internet access. In disaster situations and public health emergencies, interruptions in communication networks may delay access to online services. Offline capabilities help maintain access to important health information, allowing users to continue receiving guidance even when real-time connectivity is temporarily unavailable.
Recent studies have also highlighted the importance of offline functionality in mobile health applications. Olla and Shimskey (2015) reported that mobile health systems with offline data storage improve service continuity by allowing users to access previously stored health information during connectivity interruptions. Similarly, research on digital health applications has shown that offline data caching enhances system reliability and user accessibility, particularly in rural communities and disaster-prone areas where internet connectivity may be inconsistent (World Health Organization, 2021).
The reviewed literature demonstrates that offline mobile technologies improve the reliability and accessibility of digital health applications by ensuring continuous access to critical information during network disruptions. However, many existing heat-health applications rely heavily on continuous internet connectivity, limiting their usefulness when users lose network access during emergencies. The proposed IniTify system addresses this limitation by incorporating an Offline Data Cache that stores essential emergency information, including first-aid guidance, emergency contact details, and the user's most recent heat-risk assessment. This feature allows users to access critical safety information even when internet connectivity is temporarily unavailable, improving the system's reliability and supporting timely response during heat-related emergencies.
3.7 Synthesis of the Literature and Research Gap
The reviewed literature demonstrates that climate change has significantly increased the occurrence and severity of extreme heat events, resulting in greater risks of heat-related illnesses and emphasizing the need for effective heat-health warning systems. Existing studies consistently recognize the importance of integrating meteorological information, digital health technologies, and artificial intelligence to improve early detection and prevention of heat-related health risks. Likewise, previous research has shown that mobile health applications, Decision Tree algorithms, GPS-based location services, and emergency response technologies can enhance health monitoring, personalized recommendations, and timely intervention during medical emergencies.
Despite these advancements, several limitations remain in existing studies and applications. Many heat-health warning systems continue to provide generalized advisories based primarily on environmental conditions without considering individual risk factors such as age, health condition, activity level, and hydration status. Similarly, most mobile health applications focus on monitoring health conditions or providing health information but do not integrate official meteorological data with intelligent heat-risk assessment. Existing emergency applications also commonly require users to manually activate emergency features, which may not be practical when individuals are experiencing severe heat-related illnesses and are unable to use their mobile devices. Furthermore, limited studies have integrated personalized heat-risk assessment, automatic emergency assistance, hospital navigation, first-aid guidance, and offline functionality within a single mobile application.
The identified gaps provide the basis for the development of IniTify. Unlike existing systems, IniTify integrates official heat index data from DOST-PAGASA with individual risk factors using a Decision Tree-based heat-risk assessment model to generate personalized heat-risk classifications and precautionary recommendations. In addition, the system incorporates an Emergency Assistance System that automatically notifies designated emergency contacts, provides hospital navigation, displays first-aid guidance, and stores essential emergency information through an offline data cache when a possible heat-related emergency is detected. By combining these features into a single mobile application, IniTify aims to strengthen heat-risk preparedness, improve early intervention, and support timely emergency response among individuals exposed to extreme heat.
The reviewed literature therefore supports the need for the proposed study and highlights its potential contribution to digital health, climate adaptation, and heat-related illness prevention. Through the integration of official meteorological data, personalized risk assessment, and emergency assistance features, the study seeks to address existing limitations in current heat-health warning systems and provide a more comprehensive and practical solution for heat-risk management.

The reviewed literature emphasizes the importance of heat-health warning systems, mobile health technologies, Decision Tree algorithms, GPS-based location services, emergency assistance systems, and offline mobile technologies in improving health monitoring and emergency preparedness. Existing literature highlights the benefits of integrating environmental information with digital technologies to promote timely interventions and reduce the risks associated with extreme heat.
Despite these advancements, several gaps remain. Most literature discusses these technologies independently rather than as components of a unified heat-risk management system. III. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
RESEARCH DESIGN
This study employed the Research and Development (R&D) design, which is appropriate for designing, developing, testing, and evaluating technological innovations. The R&D approach enabled the researchers to systematically create the IniTify mobile application and assess its functionality and user acceptability.
The system was developed to integrate official heat index data from DOST-PAGASA with individual risk factors such as age, activity level, hydration status, health condition, and GPS location. These data were processed using a Decision Tree AI model to generate personalized heat-risk assessments, real-time alerts, safety recommendations, and emergency assistance. After development, the application underwent expert validation, functional testing, integration testing, and user evaluation to determine whether it met the desired quality standards. 
SAMPLING PROCEDURE AND SAMPLES
This study did not involve human respondents, survey participants, or a respondent-based sampling procedure. The researchers themselves conducted the system evaluation using predefined test cases and controlled testing scenarios. The purpose of the testing was to determine whether the implemented functions operated according to their specified expected behavior.
A total of 25 predefined test cases were used to validate the Decision Tree AI model. The cases represented different combinations of heat index, age, health condition, hydration status, and activity level. In addition, the researchers conducted repeated functional tests, performance measurements, failure-condition tests, task-based usability tests, and emergency alert and escalation scenarios. These tests provided the empirical basis for evaluating the implemented system without requiring external respondents.
DATA GATHERING METHODS
Instruments and Tools
The study will utilize the following instruments and tools for the development, testing, and evaluation of the IniTify system:
DOST-PAGASA iHeatMAP – Served as the official source of real-time heat index and weather data used for heat-risk monitoring and analysis. 
 IniTify Android Mobile Application – The primary system developed in this study, which provides personalized heat-risk assessments, real-time alerts, safety recommendations, and emergency assistance features. 
Firebase/MySQL Database – Used to store and manage user information, environmental data, and system-generated results. 
Decision Tree AI Model – Processed environmental and individual risk factors to classify users into Low, Moderate, High, or Extreme heat-risk levels. 
Android Smartphone with GPS and Internet Connectivity – Used to retrieve real-time heat index data, determine the user's location, display notifications, and activate emergency assistance features. 
Development Tools – Software such as Android Studio, the programming language used for the application, and the Firebase platform were utilized to develop, implement, and test the IniTify mobile application. 
System Validation
The developed application underwent several validation and testing procedures before final evaluation:
Functional Testing – verified whether the implemented system features performed their specified functions correctly.
Performance Testing – measured response times for selected operations under Wi-Fi, mobile-data, and offline conditions.
Reliability and Failure-Condition Testing – evaluated the application's handling of selected connectivity, permission, and offline conditions.
AI Model Validation – compared the Decision Tree model's actual classifications with the expected classifications across 25 predefined test cases.
Data Collection Procedure
To obtain the need for the study, the following procedure were undertaken by the researchers. 
The researchers reviewed related literature and studies to identify the necessary features, system requirements, and technologies for the development of the IniTify mobile application. 
The IniTify mobile application was developed using Android Studio and integrated with Firebase/MySQL. The system was configured to retrieve official heat index data from DOST-PAGASA and collect user information, including age, activity level, hydration status, health condition, and GPS location. 
The application retrieved real-time heat index data from DOST-PAGASA and combined it with the user's personal information. These data were processed using the Decision Tree AI model to classify users into Low, Moderate, High, or Extreme heat-risk levels and generate personalized recommendations and real-time alerts. 
The developed system underwent expert validation, functional testing, integration testing, and AI model validation to verify that all system components operated correctly and produced accurate heat-risk classifications. 
After successful testing and validation, the IniTify mobile application was implemented to demonstrate its capability to provide personalized heat-risk monitoring, real-time alerts, safety recommendations, and emergency assistance based on official heat index data and individual risk factors. 
DATA ANALYSIS
The collected testing data were analyzed using descriptive measures appropriate to the researcher-conducted system evaluation. For the Decision Tree AI model, the expected and actual classifications of the 25 predefined test cases were compared. The validation success rate was computed as the percentage of cases in which the actual result matched the expected result. For functional and reliability testing, successful and failed executions were summarized using frequencies and percentages. Performance results were summarized using mean completion or response times. Task-based usability was assessed using task completion, recorded errors, and completion time. Emergency alert and escalation testing was interpreted based on whether each predefined scenario produced the expected system behavior.
Validation Success Rate – used to determine the percentage of predefined AI test cases in which the actual classification matched the expected classification.
Functional Pass Rate – used to determine the percentage of functional test executions that successfully produced the expected result.
Mean Response/Completion Time – used to summarize the recorded time required for selected system operations and task completion.
Failure-Condition Success Rate – used to determine the proportion of selected failure-condition trials that were successfully handled by the application.
Scenario Pass Result – used to determine whether each predefined emergency alert and escalation scenario produced its specified expected outcome.

Four cases did not match the expected results. In Case 9 the expected classification was High. The model gave Extreme. In Case 10 the expected result was Low. The model output was High. Case 11 was expected to be High. The model said Extreme. Case 15 was expected to be High. The model again classified it as Extreme.
An important point is that in all four cases where the model failed it gave a risk classification than expected. This means the model did not underestimate risk. It overestimated it instead. While this shows a problem the table alone does not explain why these errors happened. So we cannot say which factor caused the mistakes unless further analysis is done on the model.
The 84 percent success rate shows that the Decision Tree worked well across the test cases.. It did not produce the expected result in every case. This improvement is especially needed before the model is used in real-world settings.
It is also important to understand that the 84 percent result only applies to these 25 test cases. It should not be called real-world accuracy. The test data is made up of predefined cases, not real-world data collected from actual users. The result shows how the model performed against the expected outputs in the test cases.
Overall the Decision Tree model correctly classified the heat-risk level in 21 out of 25 cases. This gives an 84 percent validation success rate. The results support using the Decision Tree as the AI-based personalized heat-risk assessment feature, in IniTify. At the time the four cases that failed show that the model needs more review and refinement to improve consistency.

V.
SUMMARY, CONCLUSIONS, AND RECOMMENDATIONS
A. Summary of Findings
The study developed IniTify, an AI-based heat-risk monitoring and personalized alert system. IniTify combines environmental heat data with health and lifestyle factors to deliver heat-risk assessments, safety advice, alerts and emergency support. The researchers tested the system using predefined test cases and controlled scenarios than real users. The evaluation covered key areas: Decision Tree validation, functional testing, performance measurement, reliability testing, task-based usability testing and emergency alert and escalation testing.
The results are presented according to the research questions of the study.
1. Environmental and Individual Factors Integrated into the System
The system integrates five factors into its heat-risk assessment. The environmental factor is the heat index. The individual factors include age, health condition, activity level and hydration status. These inputs allow the system to create a risk profile by considering both the surrounding heat conditions and the users personal characteristics.
2. Performance of the Heat-Risk Assessment, Monitoring, Alert and Safety-Support Features
2.1 Decision Tree AI Model
The Decision Tree AI model was tested using 25 predefined cases, each with combinations of heat index, age, health condition, hydration status and activity level. The model correctly classified the heat risk in 21 out of 25 cases achieving an 84% success rate. Four cases or 16% did not match the expected risk level. The heat index values tested ranged from 24°C to 41°C. Ages in the test cases ranged from 18 to 70 years old.
The results show that the Decision Tree model performed well in cases. However the four incorrect classifications highlight the need for refinement before the model can be confidently applied to broader situations.
2.2 Weather Monitoring, Heat-Index, Alert, Safety-Prompt and Recommendation Features
All six functional features—weather retrieval, heat-index display, safety recommendations, safety-prompt triggering, safety-prompt response and notifications—were tested. Each feature completed five trials with all 30 trials passing. This gives a 100% pass rate.
Performance testing measured response times under network conditions. Weather and heat-index retrieval had times of 2.52 seconds via Wi-Fi, 2.49 seconds via mobile data and 0.77 seconds offline. Personalized recommendation display took 0.86 seconds, 0.83 seconds and 0.73 seconds across the same conditions. The notification and safety-prompt triggering operation took an average of 5.75 seconds via Wi-Fi, 5.78 seconds via mobile data and 5.87 seconds offline. The paper notes that some of these measurements involve operations and should not be read as individual feature performance.
2.3 User-Support, Emergency, Navigation and Offline Features
Functional testing included authentication, user profiles, Tify check-in, emergency escalation, emergency contacts, GPS, hospital navigation, first-aid guidance, offline information and network failure handling. All 50 tests passed, resulting in a 100% success rate. This shows that the system behaved as expected during the evaluation.
Reliability testing looked at four failure scenarios: network disconnection and reconnection unavailable GPS permission, disabled notification permission and access to data while offline. Each scenario was tested five times with all 20 trials handled successfully resulting in a 100% success rate. However, the researchers noticed that notifications were sometimes duplicated. This issue was noted as needing attention.
For task-based usability testing all eight tasks were completed without errors. Task completion times varied from 0.74 seconds to 12.20 seconds with an average of 6.43 seconds. These results suggest that the tested workflows could be completed successfully by the researchers. Still because the testing was done by the research team these results should not be taken as a measure of usability for the general target population.
Emergency alert and escalation testing also passed all scenarios. These included low-risk conditions, high-risk alerts, alert acknowledgment, nonresponse, emergency situations and handling of emergency contacts. The results confirm that the emergency workflow functioned as intended.
3. Developed Decision Tree AI Model
The decision tree model used in IniTify has five input variables: heat index, age, health condition, hydration status and activity level. The heat index is the input. The other four are factors. These inputs are processed by the decision tree algorithm to produce one of four heat-risk classifications: Moderate, High or Extreme.
These four classes represent an increase, in risk. Low means the risk category. Moderate means the risk is rising and more caution is needed. High means the risk is significant and stronger preventive actions are required. Extreme means the risk category indicating a critical situation. These classifications are used to guide the system’s safety recommendations, alerts and emergency responses.
B. Conclusion
Based on the findings of the study the following conclusions were drawn:
• The developed IniTify system combined environmental and individual factors to assess heat risk on a personalized basis. The heat index was used as the factor while age, health condition, activity level and hydration status were included as individual factors. This approach allows the system to consider both the heat conditions and the personal situation of the user.
• The Decision Tree AI model performed well in most of the validation cases. Out of 25 cases it correctly classified 21 achieving an 84% success rate. This shows that the model can be reliable as the AI component of the system within the scope of the test data. However, the four incorrect classifications show that further refinement and broader testing are needed before the model can be trusted in real-world settings.
• The core functions of the system—monitoring, heat index tracking, alerts, safety prompts and recommendations—worked as expected during testing. All 30 executions of the six functions were successful. Performance data also showed how the application responded under network conditions: Wi-Fi, mobile data and offline.
• The user-support, emergency, navigation and offline features also worked correctly. Functions such as authentication, profile management, Tify check-in, emergency assistance, handling of emergency contacts, GPS, hospital navigation, first-aid guidance, access to information and handling network failures all passed all five test trials.
• The system handled the failure conditions and emergency scenarios tested by the researchers properly. All 20 reliability tests were successful. The emergency workflows followed their intended paths. However, notification duplication was observed in some cases. This technical issue should be fixed in updates to improve system reliability.
• The task-based usability test showed that the application workflows could be completed. All eight tasks were finished without errors. The average time to complete each task was 6.43 seconds. Since the test was done by researchers and not actual end users the results offer insights into usability rather than a full evaluation of how real users would interact with the system.
• The Decision Tree model created a four-level heat-risk classification system. It takes five inputs. Outputs one of four risk levels: Low Moderate, High or Extreme. These risk levels then connect directly to the app’s alerts, recommendations and safety support features.
• The study shows that it is technically possible to combine AI-based heat-risk assessment with monitoring, personalized advice, alerts, emergency help and offline support in a mobile app. However, the results should be viewed within the limits of the study. The research does not prove effectiveness, real-world accuracy or performance in every possible environment. The paper acknowledges limitations, including reliance on heat-index data, user-input information, location permissions, internet access for some features and testing only, in Tuguegarao City during the study period.
C. Recommendation
Based on the findings and limitations of the study the following recommendations are proposed:
1. Refine the Decision Tree AI model. The four validation cases that did not match their expected classifications should be reviewed to determine which decision conditions contributed to the mismatches. Additional validation cases should also be created to cover a range of heat-index values and combinations of age, health condition, hydration status and activity level.
2. Conduct AI model validation. Future researchers should evaluate the Decision Tree using a more diverse validation dataset. Where appropriate the model may also be compared with classification approaches to determine whether its performance can be improved.
3. Resolve the notification duplication issue. The observed frequent duplication of notifications should be. Corrected to prevent unnecessary repeated alerts and improve the reliability of the notification mechanism.
4. Conduct user testing. Future evaluation should include members of the intended user population rather than relying only on researcher-conducted task testing. This would provide evidence regarding usability; clarity of instructions eases of navigation and user experience.
5. Expand performance testing. Additional performance tests should be conducted using mobile devices, operating conditions, network speeds and connectivity conditions. Longer-duration testing may also help determine whether performance remains consistent during use.
6. Conduct field testing in environmental conditions. Since the current study is limited to the development and testing of the application within Tuguegarao City future researchers should evaluate the system in locations and under different heat and environmental conditions to determine whether the results remain consistent.
7. Improve emergency-assistance validation. Future studies should conduct extensive emergency simulations involving different combinations of risk levels user responses, location conditions, connectivity states and emergency-contact configurations. This would provide evidence for the reliability of the emergency escalation workflow.
8. Improve capabilities. Since some real-time features require an internet connection, future version should continue improving cached information and offline access to essential safety guidance while clearly communicating which features require connectivity.
9. Strengthen GPS and hospital information testing. Future versions should test location accuracy and hospital information across locations and connectivity conditions to ensure that emergency navigation remains useful during actual heat-related situations.
10. Maintain the system as a decision-support and early-warning tool. The application should not be presented as a diagnostic or treatment device. Future development should retain limitations and safety messaging because the system depends on environmental data, user-provided information, device permissions and network availability.
11. Standardize the applications name throughout the manuscript. The final paper uses IniTify in the title and several sections. Uses IniTify in some research questions and Results and Discussion sections. Before submission or defense the researchers should choose the application name and use it consistently throughout the title, research questions, tables, figures, discussion and conclusions. This is a format and consistency issue, not a change, to the research findings.
